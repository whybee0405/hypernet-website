# Hypernet — Brand Identity System v1.1

> **Reliable. Trustworthy. Innovative.**
> Authoritative AI reference for Hypernet brand communications, UI/UX, campaigns, documents, and asset generation.

## 1. Brand Essence

Hypernet is a South African telecoms provider for small and medium businesses that are tired of being treated like an account number. It provides connectivity and VoIP with responsive, human support.

### Core Pillars

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reliable | Uptime and consistency where the call and connection matter most. |
| Trustworthy | Straight answers, fair terms, and no hiding behind call centres or fine print. |
| Innovative | Practical modern connectivity and VoIP sized for SMEs rather than bloated enterprise packages. |

### Tagline

> Connectivity with a Human Voice.

### Positioning

Hypernet is the personal alternative to South Africa's cold, impersonal telecoms giants — a connectivity and VoIP partner for SMEs that answers the phone, fixes downtime fast, and treats every customer like a relationship rather than a ticket number.

## 2. Creative North Star — The Direct Line

> The provider who still picks up the phone.

Hypernet is the direct line back to a real person. Every visual and verbal choice should reinforce directness, warmth, practical technical competence, and a genuine human presence behind the connection.

## 3. Colour System

### Core Colours

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Hypernet Blue | #155EEF | Primary CTAs, links, active states, iconography, and highlights. |
| Hypernet Blue Deep | #0B3D91 | Headlines on white, structural authority, headers, and footers. |
| Hypernet Coral | #E8703A | Human warmth, quotes, small callouts, and limited accents. |
| Hypernet Coral Deep | #C1571F | Print emphasis and hover states on coral elements. |

### Supporting Neutrals

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| White | #FFFFFF | Dominant page and interface surface. |
| Cloud | #FAFBFC | Subtle panel and card separation. |
| Ink | #101828 | Primary body copy. |
| Slate | #667085 | Secondary text, labels, and captions. |
| Hairline | #E4E7EC | Dividers, borders, and table rules. |

### Distribution and Contrast

- Approximately 80% White or Cloud, 15% Blue, and 5% Coral.
- White leads; colour supports hierarchy rather than dominating layouts.
- Blue or coral details on white are approved.
- White type on Blue Deep is approved for authority moments.
- Never set body copy on full-coral cards.

## 4. Typography

| Role | Family | Weight | Primary use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Manrope | ExtraBold 800 | Headlines, titles, cover statements. |
| Body | Inter | Regular 400 / Medium 500 | Paragraphs, descriptions, and labels. |
| Technical | Spline Sans Mono | SemiBold 600 | Specs, plan data, metadata, and footers; uppercase only. |

### Type Scale

- Cover title: Manrope ExtraBold, 72px.
- Section header: Manrope ExtraBold, 52px.
- Card heading: Manrope ExtraBold, 26px.
- Body: Inter Regular, 16px.
- Technical metadata: Spline Sans Mono SemiBold, 11px, uppercase.
- Use no more than two weights per composition and no more than three sizes in internal documents.

## 5. Visual Principles

1. **Warmth First** — Feel human before technical, with rounded forms and generous space.
2. **White Leads, Colour Accents** — Blue structures; coral adds restrained warmth.
3. **Technology in Context** — Technology is welcome when it demonstrates a real service, installation, workflow, or customer outcome.
4. **No Generic Telecom Clichés** — Avoid wifi bars, globes, headset stock, abstract glowing cables, and decorative sci-fi infrastructure.
5. **Typographic Confidence** — Bold direct headlines and calm legible body copy.
6. **Real People, Businesses, and Technology** — Show genuine SMEs alongside authentic equipment and operational detail.
7. **Proof Over Decoration** — Every visual element should carry information or structure.

## 6. Layout and Grid

- Desktop canvas: 1440px; outer margins 72px; gutters 24px; target width 1280px.
- Mobile: four columns; outer margins 24px; gutters 16px.
- Spacing scale: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128px.
- Primary card radius: 12px. Data-table radius: 0px.
- Section divider: 1px Hairline.
- Signature accent: 2–4px Blue or Coral top border.
- Authority moment: sparing full-bleed Blue Deep with white type.
- Standard card padding: 32px.
- Major section gap: 64px. Related gap: 24px. Paragraph gap: 16px.

## 7. Component Language

### Interactive Elements

- Primary CTA: Get Connected or Talk to a Real Person; Blue background and white text.
- Badges: LOCAL SUPPORT and REAL PEOPLE, REAL FAST; Blue or Coral accents on white.

### Reliability Metrics

Display numbers use Manrope ExtraBold at 52px or larger with uppercase Spline Sans Mono captions.

- 99.9% / NETWORK UPTIME — placeholder; verify before publishing.
- <2 MIN / AVERAGE SUPPORT RESPONSE — placeholder; verify before publishing.
- 100% / SA-BASED SUPPORT TEAM — placeholder; verify before publishing.

Never present unverified figures as facts.

### Plan Tables

Use left-aligned Inter labels, right-aligned values, Hairline dividers, and no zebra striping.

| Plan | Connectivity | VoIP lines | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| SME Starter | Up to 50 Mbps | 2 lines | Business hours |
| SME Growth | Up to 100 Mbps | 5 lines | Extended hours |
| SME Pro | Up to 200 Mbps | 10 lines | Priority + after-hours |

Plan details are examples and must be confirmed before publication.

## 8. Imagery Direction

Hypernet imagery balances human connection with credible, purposeful technology. It should show both the people behind the service and the systems that keep real businesses connected.

### Approved Subjects

| Category | Direction |
|---|---|
| Real SME workplaces | Small offices, shops, workshops, logistics offices, and ecommerce operations. |
| Genuine conversations | Real support calls, face-to-face guidance, and technicians explaining work. |
| Connectivity in use | Laptops, VoIP phones, POS systems, mobile devices, and cloud workflows serving a real task. |
| Network equipment | Business routers, switches, access points, fibre termination, patch panels, and tidy network cabinets. |
| Installation and testing | Technicians installing, configuring, testing, labelling, or troubleshooting equipment. |
| Monitoring and interfaces | Network-status dashboards, call-quality tools, and support interfaces with realistic, legible context. |
| Local context | Recognisably South African township, suburban, and city SME environments. |

### Photography Rules

- Use warm, natural or balanced practical light; technology may be crisp and precise without becoming cold or futuristic.
- Show authentic operations rather than staged corporate stock.
- Pair wider environmental views with close details of hands, devices, cabling, interfaces, and equipment.
- Keep installations tidy, safe, credible, and technically plausible.
- Technology must explain a service, workflow, proof point, or customer benefit.
- Human presence is encouraged but not mandatory in every image when the technical subject is meaningful on its own.

### Avoid

- Generic call-centre headset stock.
- Floating wifi symbols, globe graphics, or generic telecom icon montages.
- Abstract glowing cables, neon data tunnels, holograms, or cold sci-fi composites.
- Decorative server racks or hardware with no clear relationship to Hypernet's SME offer.
- Messy, unsafe, implausible, or visibly outdated installations unless documenting a before-and-after story.
- Technology imagery that could belong to any telecom brand and carries no operational meaning.

## 9. Brand Voice

| Attribute | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Warm | Speak like a person, never a policy document or script. |
| Direct | Say what is meant without jargon-padded non-answers. |
| Reliable | Stay calm, clear, and steady, especially during an issue. |

### Terminology

| Preferred | Avoid |
|---|---|
| A real person on the line | A dedicated support agent |
| We'll sort it | Your ticket has been logged |
| Straight pricing, no surprises | Competitive market-related rates |
| Your local Hypernet team | Our customer service department |

Technical language is acceptable when it helps a customer make a decision or understand an issue. Explain it in plain language.

## 11. Copywriting by Persona

### Retail — Plain-Spoken and Reassuring

- Value: uptime that protects trading hours and a real person when something breaks.
- CTA: Talk to a Real Person Today.
- Avoid unnecessary bandwidth and latency jargon.

### Professional Services — Professional and Steady

- Value: dependable VoIP quality that protects client relationships.
- CTA: Get a Straight Quote.
- Never promise zero downtime; state verified SLA terms honestly.

### Logistics and Operations — Practical and No-Nonsense

- Value: right-sized connectivity and VoIP without enterprise overkill.
- CTA: Find Your Plan.
- Avoid vague enterprise-solutions language.

### Growing SMEs — Encouraging and Partner-Minded

- Value: connectivity that grows with the business.
- CTA: Grow With Hypernet.
- Avoid distant corporate-solutions language.

## 12. Logo System

Two directions remain in development.

- **The Conversation Mark:** speech-bubble form merged with a signal swoosh; leads with human positioning and nods to VoIP.
- **The Connection Point:** H monogram with a small gap suggesting two joining points; suited to favicons, app icons, and vehicle signage.

Final selection is pending client sign-off.

### Rules for the Final Mark

- Clear space: 50% of the mark width on every side.
- Minimum width: 30mm print and 100px digital.
- Never distort, recolour, shadow, or place on busy low-contrast backgrounds.
- Use a white reverse variant on dark backgrounds.

## 13. Brand in Action

- Corporate stationery: cards, letterheads, invoices.
- Office signage: warm, uncluttered environmental branding.
- Vehicle branding: white vehicles with Blue and Coral details.
- Digital presence: website, social templates, email signatures, and advertising.
- Support materials: SLA documents and onboarding packs.
- Client reports: account and usage summaries.
- Technical content: installation photography, network diagrams, service-status communication, product explainers, and equipment guides that follow the imagery rules.

## 14. Compliance Checklist

1. Human first; never sound scripted.
2. Technology must be purposeful, authentic, and connected to a real SME outcome.
3. Do not use generic telecom clichés or decorative sci-fi imagery.
4. Maintain accessible contrast; never place white body type on raw Coral.
5. Maintain final-logo clear space and approved lockups.
6. Use single-colour Blue on white for low-cost print applications.
7. Use no more than three type sizes in internal documents.
8. Publish only verified uptime, response, SLA, plan, and support figures.
9. Tell the founder story in third person without using the founder's name publicly.
10. Obtain Hypernet sign-off before publication.

## AI Usage Rules

- Treat this document as the primary source of truth.
- Preserve the positioning, tagline, palette, type roles, and voice unless explicitly instructed otherwise.
- Never invent metrics, SLAs, prices, coverage, plan specifications, certifications, or customer claims.
- Mark uncertain claims as TO VERIFY or PLACEHOLDER.
- Balance human imagery with credible technology; do not force a person into every technical composition.
- Prefer specific South African SME context over generic global telecom imagery.
- Use proof, clarity, and operational context rather than decoration.
