# Assumptions, unresolved facts and South African lawyer review

Prepared 19 October 2026. These are review drafts, not an opinion that the site complies with the law. No pages, banner controls or analytics settings have been published or changed by this drafting work.

## Confirmed context and inspected evidence

The owner supplied:

- Company: Hypernet (Pty) Ltd; registration 2018/376463/07.
- Company address: 33 Adrienne Street, Sandown, Gauteng, 2191, South Africa.
- Emails are collected only for enquiries, not newsletters or marketing.
- Custom analytics measures site visits and CTA clicks; its data is hosted in South Africa.
- The audience is mainly South African and the operator is a registered company.

The current contact form and handler show collection of name, business, email, telephone, interest, message and source, with lead status in the CMS. The required fields in P5 follow the inspected form. These are observations of website code, not confirmation of all company-wide processing. The existing site lists hello@hypernet.co.za and 011 568 4192, but those have not been confirmed as privacy/legal-notice channels.

**Important inconsistencies:** the current live privacy-page source says analytics is not used; the owner now confirms custom analytics. It also states a two-year enquiry retention period without evidence of an implemented deletion process. The existing footer/contact source contains a Kramerville office address, different from the company address supplied. A trading address may differ from a company address, but the labels and details need verification. These drafts do not change those live statements.

## Separate list B — South African lawyer review before publication

1. **Correct entity disclosures and contracting party.** Confirm the company details, address labels, VAT and contact disclosures, notice/service address and authority to contract. Determine the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act disclosures and online-transaction rules applicable to the actual site and sales flow.
2. **POPIA scope and lawful processing.** Review data minimisation, each purpose and lawful basis, indirect sources, compatible further use and the collection notice. Decide whether every required contact field is justified; an email enquiry should not silently authorise broader processing.
3. **Custom analytics in detail.** Assess identifiers, linkability, profiling, raw IPs and referrers, tracking across sessions/sites, and any collection before consent. Confirm whether the proposed opt-in model is appropriate. South African data hosting alone does not establish compliance or prove anonymity.
4. **Consent and rejection mechanics.** Review whether consent is voluntary, specific and informed and how evidence and withdrawal are handled. The text is deliberately conservative; do not present an EU cookie-rule template as automatically required by POPIA. Review any additional foreign rules if users in other jurisdictions are actually targeted or monitored.
5. **Notice accuracy and contradictions.** Replace the current “no analytics” statement once approved and align every form notice, banner, privacy page, vendor list and retention statement. Check the company/trading address distinction. Review customer case studies, testimonials and imagery that the repository identifies as example material awaiting approval.
6. **Information Officer and PAIA.** Confirm the correct Information Officer, registration/delegations, contact channels, any impact-assessment/compliance responsibilities, and the company's PAIA manual/publication/request obligations. Do not assume a registration is already completed.
7. **Operators and other recipients.** Determine each party's role; review appropriate written terms, confidentiality, security, purpose restrictions, incident escalation and deletion. Verify which quotation/coverage checks disclose enquiry data to separate responsible parties.
8. **International transfers.** Review POPIA section 72 for all cross-border access and transfers, including email, backups and support. Determine the actual protections and any additional authorisation requirements if special information or children's data is involved.
9. **Retention and rights.** Approve period-by-period schedules and legal holds; make sure the business can execute access, correction, objections and deletion/restriction requests. Check identity verification, PAIA interfaces, permissible fees and response periods. Do not promise a universal GDPR-style 30-day deadline or portability right without a proper basis.
10. **Security and incident response.** Review risk-appropriate controls and the section 22 notification process, recipients and timing. No blanket GDPR-style 72-hour deadline has been inserted. Confirm contracts support timely escalation and legally appropriate communication.
11. **Email use.** Confirm enquiry replies and follow-ups remain within the stated purpose. If marketing is introduced, separately review section 69, the Regulator's direct-marketing guidance, consent records, any proposed existing-customer exception, opt-outs and suppression records. Neither a cookie choice nor an enquiry is general marketing permission.
12. **Children, sensitive information and automation.** Verify the assumptions, handling of accidental submissions and any applicable special-information/children/prior-authorisation requirements. Review any profiling or significant automated decision-making outside the disclosed simple analytics.
13. **Consumer law and fairness.** Determine the Consumer Protection Act's application to individuals and qualifying juristic persons; “business customers” is not a blanket exemption. Review plain language, unfair terms, liability limitations, conspicuous notices and any cooling-off, cancellation, renewal and refund rights applicable to the transaction.
14. **Actual paid-service documents.** These drafts are not a master service agreement. Review coverage, installation, equipment ownership, VAT, usage limits, billing, arrears, cancellation, renewal, acceptable use, SLAs, service credits, portability and outage handling against the services actually sold. Determine relevant telecommunications/sector licensing, disclosure and complaint requirements.
15. **Contract formation and changes.** Review quotation acceptance, e-signature/electronic records, version evidence, notice before acceptance and order-of-precedence. Decide which website provisions can be enforced without affirmative acceptance; do not rely on passive browsing as a universal solution.
16. **Intellectual property and advertising substantiation.** Verify licences and permitted reuse, third-party brand claims, customer permissions, illustrative images and substantiation of performance, partnership, availability and price statements. The terms cannot cure misleading or unapproved content.
17. **Liability, disputes and remedies.** Tailor exclusions, any future caps/indemnities and force-majeure clauses to applicable law and insurance. Review complaint procedures, forum provisions and external consumer/sector remedies. Preserve mandatory rights; do not assume a savings sentence validates an otherwise unfair term.
18. **Publication readiness and wider processing.** Verify the final effective date, working privacy/complaint contacts, settings links, notice visibility, accessibility, consent-control test evidence and ownership of future updates. Identify any separate customer, employee, call-recording, communications-service or other privacy notices needed. Recheck applicable law and guidance when approving publication.

## Sources consulted

Checked 19 October 2026. These provide the legal frame for review; the drafts do not assert that every provision applies identically to every visitor or service.

- [Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 — official government publication](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act). Review especially sections 11, 14, 18–24, 55, 69, 71 and 72 and the applicable regulations.
- [Information Regulator: direct-marketing guidance](https://inforegulator.org.za/guidance-notes/) and [the published guidance note](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GUIDANCE-NOTE-ON-DIRECT-MARKETING-IN-TERMS-OF-THE-PROTECTION-OF-PERSONAL-INFORMATION-ACT-4-OF-2013-POPIA.pdf). Relevant if enquiry data is later proposed for marketing.
- [Information Regulator: complaint/contact channels](https://inforegulator.org.za/contact-us/).
- [Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002 — official government page](https://www.gov.za/documents/electronic-communications-and-transactions-act). Counsel should check the current applicable text and transaction-specific requirements.
- [Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 — official government page](https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act). Counsel should confirm current law, thresholds, exclusions and transaction-specific application.
